# Brand

Working document for Call Pilot's brand: idea, personality, voice, naming and visual direction. It builds on [POSITIONING.md](POSITIONING.md) and feeds [DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md](DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md), which turns the chosen direction into tokens.

- **Status:** v0.1 draft, 2026-11-11.
- **Decided:** the brand idea, personality and voice. Visual direction **A, "Control tower"**. **Light theme only** for now.
- **Not decided yet:** the name, exact colors and the typeface. We'll test one commercial and one free Hebrew face, then choose. Nothing here is applied to the site until it's approved.

---

## 1. Brand idea

**Calm control over every customer conversation.**

A good pilot doesn't make noise. They keep the flight steady, see everything on the instruments, handle the routine automatically and take the controls when it matters. Call Pilot does the same for customer communication. AI agents handle the routine on phone, WhatsApp and SMS, everything is visible in one place, and your team steps in exactly when needed.

The aviation idea is a quiet undercurrent, not a theme. No cockpit graphics, plane icons or "ready for takeoff" puns. It shows up as steadiness, clarity and precision.

## 2. Personality: calm and precise

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Calm: confident without raising our voice | Hyped: "revolutionary", exclamation marks, countdowns |
| Precise: specific numbers, exact words, clear scope | Vague: "seamless", "next-gen", "AI-powered everything" |
| Trustworthy: we say what it does and what it doesn't | Overpromising: "replaces your team", "never wrong" |
| Local: Israeli, direct, at home in Hebrew | Translated: global copy with Hebrew poured in |
| Serious about operations: built for real teams | Playful-startup: mascots, slang for its own sake |

**Test:** would a calm, senior head of operations say this sentence out loud to a peer? If not, rewrite it.

## 3. Voice and tone

### Principles

1. **Say the specific thing.** "Every call ends with a summary in Fireberry" beats "smart CRM integration".
2. **Short sentences, plain verbs.** One idea per sentence.
3. **Lead with the customer's outcome**, then how it works.
4. **Admit limits plainly.** It builds trust: "When the agent isn't sure, it says so and transfers."
5. **Never shout.** No exclamation marks in product or marketing copy. No all caps.

### Hebrew (primary)

These follow the Hebrew copy rules in DESIGN_PRINCIPLES §8:

- Address readers in the plural ("אתם", "התחילו", "תאמו הדגמה").
- Use spoken, professional Hebrew: what a sharp Israeli ops manager says, not a translated brochure.
- Use loanwords customers actually use (AI, CRM, דשבורד). Don't force rare coinages.
- Write the Hebrew first. The English is adapted from the idea, never translated line by line.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| הפתרון המהפכני מבוסס הבינה המלאכותית לשירות לקוחות! | סוכני AI שעונים ללקוחות שלכם בטלפון ובווטסאפ, בעברית. |
| אנו מתחייבים לחוויית לקוח חלקה | כל שיחה נרשמת, מסוכמת ומגיעה לאדם הנכון. |
| הצטרפו למהפכה | תאמו הדגמה |
| מערכת חכמה שמחליפה את המוקד | הסוכן מטפל בשגרה. הצוות שלכם מטפל במה שצריך אדם. |

### English (secondary)

Same voice: calm, specific, sentence case. Use British or American spelling consistently (to decide; default American).

### Vocabulary

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| AI agent / סוכן AI | bot / בוט (feels cheap), "digital human" |
| conversation / שיחה | interaction, touchpoint |
| customer / לקוח | user (for the end customer), contact |
| hand off to your team / מעביר לצוות | escalate (internal jargon) |
| book a demo / תאמו הדגמה | request a consultation, contact sales |

## 4. Naming

### The current name: Call Pilot

#### Strengths

- Carries the brand idea (pilot, autopilot, calm control) and pairs naturally with "טייס אוטומטי" in Hebrew copy.
- Easy to say in both languages.

#### Problems

- "Call" narrows the product to phone. We sell voice plus WhatsApp and SMS.
- In Hebrew, "פיילוט" usually means a trial or pilot project, so "קול פיילוט" can read as "a trial of calls".
- Generic English compound, hard to own. "CallPilot" has existed as a telephony/voicemail product name, so a trademark check is needed.
- Sounds like a tool more than a platform for mid-to-large buyers.

### Criteria for a final name

1. Works and is pronounceable in both Hebrew and English, and is easy to spell after hearing it on a call.
2. Not limited to one channel (not "call", "voice" or "phone" only).
3. Calm, serious and short: 1 to 3 syllables ideally.
4. Ownable: trademark clear in Israel (and the EU/US for later), and a usable domain (.com, .ai or .co.il).
5. Doesn't collide with a competitor: avoid names close to Daber, Yappr, Aircall or Voicenter.

### Shortlist to discuss

All of these are unchecked for trademark and domain.

| Name | Hebrew | Idea | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kesher** | קשר | "Connection"; also "radio contact" in aviation and the army, so it keeps the pilot undercurrent | Very common word, so ownership is hard; may need a qualifier |
| **Maane** | מענה | "Response"; the exact word Israelis use for answering customers (מענה טלפוני) | English spelling is awkward (Maane / Ma'ane) |
| **Kav** | קו | "Line", as in a phone line and a line of communication | Short and strong, but common |
| **Tower** | טאואר | The control tower: calm, total visibility, guiding every flight | English-first; less natural in Hebrew copy |
| **Pilot** | פיילוט | Drops "Call" and keeps the idea | Same Hebrew "trial" issue; very hard to own |

**Recommendation:** keep "Call Pilot" as the working name while the site is designed (the design shouldn't depend on it). Shortlist 2 or 3 names after trademark and domain checks, and decide before any public launch.

## 5. Visual direction (to choose)

### Fixed principles, whatever we choose

- **Hebrew-first, RTL-first.** The Hebrew typeface leads; Latin is matched to it.
- **The product is the hero image.** The real customer dashboard and conversation timeline, not abstract AI art.
- **Calm and precise:** generous space, few colors, one accent used sparingly, motion that settles rather than bounces (DESIGN_PRINCIPLES §7).
- **Distinct from competitors:**
  - Avoid Aircall's deep green on cream with serif accents.
  - Avoid the category's dark-mode neon, gradient orbs, particle waves and waveform clichés.
  - Avoid a generic SaaS card grid.
- **Accessible by default:** AA contrast in both themes, no meaning carried by color alone.

### Direction A: "Control tower" (chosen)

- **Feel:** cool daylight, high clarity, instrument-panel precision.
- **Colors:** pale cool neutrals (near-white with a hint of blue-grey), deep ink-navy text and one clear signal accent for actions and live states. The accent should be warm, like a runway light, against the cool base.
- **Look:** big quiet type, thin precise rules where they carry information, the product UI framed like a well-lit instrument panel.
- **Motion:** live states (a call in progress, a summary being written) carry the movement; the rest of the page stays still.
- **Why:** it matches "calm and precise", reads as serious enough for mid-to-large buyers, and is the opposite of both Aircall's green-on-cream and the neon AI look.

### Direction B: "Israeli daylight"

- **Feel:** warm, human, local.
- **Colors:** sunlit off-white and soft stone greys, a deep olive or blue-green text color and a warm accent.
- **Look:** photography of real Israeli businesses and people next to the product UI.
- **Motion:** slower and softer.
- **Why:** strongest against "it sounds like a robot" and for SMBs.
- **Risk:** drifts toward generic warm SaaS, and real photography costs time and money to do well.

### Direction C: "Signal"

- **Feel:** confident and graphic.
- **Colors:** a white base with large saturated color fields (one strong brand color, such as a deep cobalt) as section backgrounds.
- **Look:** oversized Hebrew type as the main graphic element, with the product shown in crisp frames.
- **Motion:** bolder type and scroll moments.
- **Why:** most memorable.
- **Risk:** louder than "calm"; it needs restraint to stay precise.

### Typography candidates (to test in Hebrew first)

Choose one family with real Hebrew and Latin designs, or a Hebrew face with a carefully matched Latin face.

| Typeface | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Almoni | Commercial (AlefAlefAlef) | Hebrew and Latin; precise, neutral, many weights; licence cost |
| Ploni | Commercial (AlefAlefAlef) | Hebrew and Latin; friendlier, rounder; licence cost |
| Narkiss family | Commercial (Fontef) | Distinctive Israeli character; test legibility at small sizes |
| IBM Plex Sans Hebrew | Free (OFL) | Technical and precise, pairs with IBM Plex Sans; well hinted |
| Noto Sans Hebrew | Free (OFL) | Safe fallback; less character |

Avoid Heebo, Rubik and Assistant as the primary face. They're the default on a large share of Israeli sites and would make the brand look templated.
