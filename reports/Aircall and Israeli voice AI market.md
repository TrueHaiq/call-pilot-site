# Win Israel where Aircall only translates

Aircall has become a credible but shallow competitor in Israel. It now sells an AI-first "customer communications platform" with AI Voice Agents for inbound and outbound calls, AI messaging on SMS and WhatsApp, AI copilots for human reps, and MCP-based actions inside CRMs. It added **Hebrew to its voice agents only in September 2026**. It has no Hebrew site, no shekel pricing, no Israeli templates and no visible local team. In Israel, at least a dozen local vendors (Yappr, Daber, Voicenter, Reshetcall, PayCall/Bot10, AIVA, Fixer and others) already sell Hebrew AI phone agents with the same pitch: native Hebrew, 24/7, no missed calls, CRM sync, human handoff. Almost none of them publish shekel prices, name real customers or show a real dashboard. So "best Hebrew voice AI" is a crowded claim that nobody has proven. Globally, the pages that persuade let the buyer hear the agent within seconds. Pricing is splitting into cheap per-minute developer platforms ($0.07–$0.31/min), flat SMB bundles ($49–$299/month) and unpriced enterprise contracts. Israeli law adds four hard requirements that most local competitors ignore: Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 (first fine in July 2026), wiretap and recording practice, the anti-spam law's consent rule for automated outbound, and IS 5568 web accessibility. Taken together, Call Pilot's opening is to be the one Israeli player that combines Aircall-grade product scope (agents, inbox/CRM, analytics, automations) with Hebrew quality it can prove, local pricing and local compliance. "Hebrew AI agent" alone is not a position; it is the entry ticket.

## Aircall now sells AI-led conversations, with the phone system as the hook

Aircall has moved its category label three times: "phone and communication platform" (2023), "customer communication and intelligence platform" (2024), and now "the AI-powered customer communications platform" for "sales and support teams" at "SMBs & Enterprise" ([Aircall](https://aircall.io/)). Its homepage tells the story as a three-step maturity ladder, **TEAM-LED → AI-ASSISTED → AI-LED**, which lets it upsell its installed base of phone-seat customers into AI ([Aircall](https://aircall.io/)). The company is large and profitable: **24,000+ businesses, 250+ integrations, ARR above $175M in April 2025** and reportedly above $200M by mid-2026, with a $1B valuation from its 2021 Series D ([Aircall LLM info](https://aircall.io/llm-info/); [Aircall ARR post](https://aircall.io/blog/news/aircall-surpasses-175-million-arr/); [Ecosistema Startup](https://ecosistemastartup.com/aircall-200m-arr-adquiere-startup-espanola-piper-ai-en-2026/)). In 2026 it made its first two acquisitions: **Vogent** (voice AI, turn-taking models) in May and **Piper AI** (revenue intelligence and sales workflows) in June. These point to "best-in-class voice" plus a push into revenue workflows ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260506396746/en/Aircall-Acquires-Vogent-to-Advance-Its-AI-Voice-Agent-Built-Natively-into-Business-Phones); [Aircall: Piper AI](https://aircall.io/en-gb/blog/news/aircall-piper-ai/)). Its CEO's main argument is that a voice specialist beats generalist CX suites, because AI voice works best "not when it is added as a feature by a generalist CX platform" ([Aircall Vogent release](https://aircall.io/blog/news/aircall-vogent/)). A local player can make the same argument against Aircall, one level down.

The product has four layers. Call Pilot has to match the first three to be taken seriously:

Two structural facts matter most. First, **Aircall is not a CRM**: contacts are shared or synced from HubSpot, Salesforce and others, and the "customer record" lives elsewhere ([Pricing](https://aircall.io/pricing/)). A product that ships its own CRM dashboard of calls and customers is therefore different in kind from Aircall, not a copy. Second, Aircall now sells the voice agent **standalone, "with or without a phone system"**, from a $99/month minimum ([Pricing](https://aircall.io/pricing/)). The automation layer has a gap: no native visual workflow or trigger builder was confirmed, so automation runs through AI Actions, CRM autofill and third-party tools such as Zapier. ## Aircall's Israel offer is a translation, not a localisation

Aircall's Hebrew support is real but recent and thin. Hebrew was one of **13 languages added on September 22, 2026**. Agent templates exist only "in English-speaking markets", and the free browser/phone trial of pre-built agents is limited to US and Canadian accounts ([Sept 2026 updates](https://aircall.io/blog/news/ai-voice-agents-sept26/); [AI Voice Agent page](https://aircall.io/products/ai/voice-agent/)). AI Assist, the transcription and summary layer for human reps, "verifies" only seven European languages, and the sources did not say whether Hebrew is among its 17 best-effort languages ([Aircall support](https://support.aircall.io/en-gb/articles/28733901791133)). Aircall lists Israel as "Eligible" for numbers, apparently local numbers only and not toll-free or mobile ([Aircall countries](https://aircall.io/countries/)). Its pricing gives unlimited domestic outbound only in the US and Canada, with international rates "contact for rates" ([Pricing](https://aircall.io/pricing/)). Regional data storage launched in October 2026 covers the EU, Australia and the US. There is no Israeli region, and the region is fixed at account creation ([Aircall regional data storage](https://aircall.io/en-gb/blog/news/regional-data-storage/)). No Hebrew site, ILS pricing, Israeli office or Israeli case study turned up. A +972 sales number on a third-party directory is unverified ([GetHuman](https://gethuman.com/phone-number/Aircall/sales/AZT1vZ)).

Aircall's general weaknesses add to this. Reviews repeatedly cite **call drops and quality problems "especially on international calls"**, unresponsive support, overpromised integrations, and the **three-licence minimum** as the most common pricing complaint ([G2](https://g2.com/products/aircall/reviews?page=5); [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/184709/Aircall/reviews/); [CloudTalk review roundup](https://www.cloudtalk.io/phone-system-reviews/aircall/), a competitor source). Trustpilot sentiment is "mixed", with frequent customer-service complaints ([Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/aircall.io)). CloudTalk already attacks Aircall on price in Hebrew ([CloudTalk HE](https://www.cloudtalk.io/he/blog/aircall-alternative/)). In fairness to Aircall, Hebrew quality on top of Vogent's turn-taking models could be good, and nobody has published Israeli user reviews of it yet because the feature is about two months old. The claim to make is that Aircall's Hebrew is new, generic and unsupported locally. It is not true that Aircall lacks Hebrew.

### Aircall's brand and pages leave the "hear it" moment open

Aircall's 2026 brand is **deep forest green (#008F6D) on warm paper (#FFFEFA)**, with orange and indigo accents, Fellix sans for headings, a Garamond serif accent and JetBrains Mono uppercase eyebrows. Imagery is static product screenshots on pastel or dark "aurora" gradients ([aircall.io CSS](https://aircall.io/)). The tone is calm and benefit-led: "Customer communications, evolved with AI…", "Where talk meets action.", "Know your cost before the first call." ([Aircall](https://aircall.io/); [AI Voice Agent page](https://aircall.io/products/ai/voice-agent/)). The homepage is weak as a conversion page: one testimonial with no company named, a five-logo bar, no FAQ, no ROI numbers, and **no video, audio or interactive demo** anywhere, including the AI Voice Agent page ([Aircall](https://aircall.io/); [AI Voice Agent page](https://aircall.io/products/ai/voice-agent/)). The funnel is dual: "Try for free" for self-serve plus "Talk to sales".

### Hebrew quality is claimed by everyone and benchmarked by no one

Hebrew is genuinely harder for voice AI than English because of "rich morphology, unvocalized script, and varied accents", and because most engines were trained on English first ([Reshetcall blog](https://reshetcall.co.il/en/blog/hebrew-voice-bot/)). Local vendors agree on the quality markers: **response under about 800 ms**, reliable barge-in and turn-taking, re-asking rather than guessing, and handling slang, background noise and local names ([DNAI](https://dnai.co.il/blog/ai-voice-agent-guide.html); [Fixer](https://yourfixerai.com/blog/ai-phone-answering-hebrew)). The building blocks are getting better and more available. ivrit.ai has released open Hebrew Whisper models trained on about 3,300 hours of speech and runs public leaderboards for Hebrew transcription and TTS ([ivrit.ai paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.08720); [leaderboard](https://ivrit-ai-hebrew-transcription-leaderboard.hf.space/); [TTS Arena Hebrew](https://ivrit-ai-tts-arena-hebrew.hf.space/about)). Soniox claims 1.25% Hebrew WER against ElevenLabs' 3.16% in its own benchmark ([Soniox](https://soniox.com/compare-stt/soniox-vs-elevenlabs/hebrew)). Hebrew TTS was weak until recently: March 2025 samples showed ElevenLabs multilingual v2 producing unintelligible Hebrew ([Hebrew TTS samples](https://danielrosehill-hebrew-tts-samples.static.hf.space/index.html)). Phonikud's diacritization work targets this problem directly ([phonikud](https://phonikud.github.io/)). No independent end-to-end benchmark compares Hebrew agents across vendors. That absence is both the market's credibility problem and Call Pilot's opportunity. Code-switching is barely addressed. Daber switches only when the caller does, and Edesy claims Hebrew–English mixing ([Daber](https://daber.ai/blog/daber-vs-reshetcall); [Edesy](https://edesy.in/blog/hebrew-voice-ai-complete-guide-2026.md)). No one convincingly claims Arabic or Russian alongside Hebrew, although both are large Israeli caller populations. The population share is general knowledge and was not sourced in this research.

### Israeli buyers buy missed-call relief, WhatsApp and done-for-you setup

Early adopters are appointment- and lead-driven businesses: clinics, law firms, insurance agents, real estate, restaurants, e-commerce, garages and home services. Municipal call centres sit at the enterprise end ([Yappr](https://goyappr.com/en); [DNAI](https://dnai.co.il/blog/ai-voice-agent-guide.html); [Daber](https://daber.ai/blog/daber-vs-reshetcall)). The core pain is described bluntly. One line reads "an unanswered call creates no line in any report"; another says voicemail is "where leads quietly die". A clinic case cites **34% of calls unanswered, clustered 8:00–10:00 and after 18:00** ([DNAI](https://dnai.co.il/blog/ai-voice-agent-guide.html)). Vendors name evenings, weekends and Shabbat explicitly ([AIVA](https://aiva.co.il/en/)). WhatsApp is expected, not optional. Fixer sends a WhatsApp within seconds of a missed call and shares memory across phone and WhatsApp, and Async, Daber and Yappr all bundle it ([Fixer](https://yourfixerai.com/blog/ai-phone-answering-hebrew); [async.co.il](https://async.co.il/)). The buying motion is demo-led and done-for-you: a proposal within 24 hours, free managed setup, fixed quotes, or a dedicated project manager ([AIVA](https://aiva.co.il/en/); [Yappr](https://goyappr.com/en); [Daber comparison](https://daber.ai/blog/daber-vs-reshetcall)). Local trust signals are concrete: Israeli phone numbers on the page, a Hebrew-speaking support team, named people in testimonials who have been customers for years, and regulatory badges such as a Ministry of Communications licence ([Yappr blog](https://goyappr.com/en/blog/israel-voice-ai-platform-comparison); [PayCall](https://www.paycall.co.il/%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%9F-%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%99-%D7%97%D7%9B%D7%9D)). The research found no data on Israeli market size, adoption rates or B2B payment norms.

## Four Israeli laws turn compliance into a sales asset

Israeli regulation is stricter in practice than most local vendors' marketing suggests. **Amendment 13 to the Privacy Protection Law took effect on 14 August 2025.** It gave the Privacy Protection Authority administrative fines and investigative powers, requires large-scale data holders to appoint a privacy officer, adds personal liability for management, and allows compensation without proof of damage ([RSM Israel](https://www.rsm.global/israel/he/node/228); [Meitar](https://meitar.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/AMENDMENT-13-TO-THE-PRIVACY-PROTECTION-LAW-IS-ON-THE-HORIZON-HEB.pdf)). The first fine under it, **₪256,000 against the Meuhedet health fund on 21 July 2026**, shows that enforcement is now real ([Shibolet](https://www.shibolet.com/?p=26776)). For voice AI, **recordings and transcripts are themselves a personal-data database**, so they need data mapping, a legal basis, data minimisation, and a retention and deletion policy ([DNAI](https://dnai.co.il/blog/ai-voice-agent-guide.html)). Recording a call you are party to is permitted under the Wiretap Law, and telling the caller at the start is accepted practice. Having the agent identify itself as a digital assistant is recommended but not proven to be required by law ([DNAI](https://dnai.co.il/blog/ai-voice-agent-guide.html); [Reshetcall](https://reshetcall.co.il/en/blog/ai-call-transcription/)).

The anti-spam law (Communications Law §30A) is the main risk for outbound. Advertising through an "automatic dialing system" needs **prior consent**, given in writing, by SMS, email, online or in a recorded call. There is a narrow exception for existing customers. Every message must offer a way to opt out. Damages reach **₪1,000 per message without proof of harm**, and violations are also a criminal offence ([Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פיצוי_בגין_משלוח_דברי_פרסומת_ללא_הסכמה_של_הנמען_(חוק_הספאם))). Service calls about existing bookings are treated differently from marketing calls ([DNAI](https://dnai.co.il/blog/ai-voice-agent-guide.html)). Whether a conversational AI dialer counts as an "automatic dialing system" is unsettled in the sources, so the safe assumption is yes. Finally, any public-facing website or app must meet **IS 5568 (WCAG-based, level AA)** and publish an accessibility statement, with fines up to ₪150,000 plus compensation of up to ₪50,000 without proof of damage ([Kol Zchut accessibility](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הנגשת_אתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות); [SII](https://www.sii.org.il/he/lobby/standardization/standard-page?id=6753)). Card payments taken by phone need a dedicated secure route ([DNAI](https://dnai.co.il/blog/ai-voice-agent-guide.html)), and Aircall itself has no automatic PCI redaction, only pause/resume recording ([donneespersonnelles.fr](https://donneespersonnelles.fr/aircall-rgpd)). Not yet verified: primary gov.il texts, any Israeli AI-specific disclosure rule, and whether IS 5568 is moving to WCAG 2.1/2.2.

## Global leaders win by letting buyers hear the agent

Category pages split into three groups. **SMB answering services** (Rosie, Goodcall, Smith.ai) use plain language, publish prices and offer a number to call. **Developer platforms** (Retell, Vapi, Bland) offer live demo calls and per-minute rates. **Enterprise "agentic CX"** players (Sierra, Parloa, PolyAI, and now Synthflow, which moved to annual enterprise contracts only) use abstract headlines, offer only "Book a demo" and show compliance badge walls ([Synthflow pricing](https://synthflow.ai/pricing); [Sierra](https://sierra.ai); [PolyAI](https://poly.ai)). Synthflow's move upmarket leaves the SMB-to-mid-market segment that wants a real dashboard and CRM less crowded than before.

The claims are commoditised: "sounds human", "24/7", "never miss a call", a latency figure (~0.5 s Synthflow, under 500 ms Vapi, under 300 ms PolyAI), "live in weeks", an integrations wall and a badge row ([Retell](https://www.retellai.com); [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai); [Vapi](https://vapi.ai); [PolyAI](https://poly.ai)). What separates pages is how they **prove** the product. Rosie puts a live phone number in the header with per-industry audio clips and live usage counters. Vapi has an in-browser "Start call" and the vanity number 1-844-HEY-VAPI. Retell asks the visitor to pick a use case and then calls them. Smith.ai offers **two numbers, one AI and one human**, for the same fictional law firm, followed by an SMS confirmation that shows the after-call workflow. Bland publishes an **"Audio Realism Bench"** that scores its voices against competitors. Parloa puts an ROI calculator next to "Book a demo". Synthflow shows a QA panel going from 22/40 to 40/40 passing test calls. Goodcall has a meta-agent ("Gabi") that builds your agent, and a dashboard section headlined "See every call, and what it's worth." ## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we compete with Aircall?" to "how do we become the reference point in a market where everyone sounds the same?" Aircall brings the scope and the brand but treats Israel as one of 38 languages. Local vendors bring Hebrew but sell it as an agency service, with no product breadth, transparent pricing or proof. The gap between the two is a product-grade, Israel-native platform that shows its Hebrew working instead of claiming it, prices in shekels, and treats Amendment 13 and the spam law as features. Two risks follow. Aircall's Vogent-powered Hebrew could get good quickly. And falling per-minute prices (Aircall roughly $0.49 → $0.29 within months, Edesy at a few rupees) mean any advantage built on voice quality or price alone will shrink. The lasting advantage is local depth: Israeli integrations, WhatsApp-first workflows, compliance tooling, Hebrew support, and named Israeli proof.

## Implications for Call Pilot

*This section applies the findings above to the client's context: "Call Pilot" (working name), an Aircall-like product focused mainly on Israel, targeting SMBs now and mid-to-large businesses long term, aiming to be "the best AI voice caller in Hebrew and the best AI customer communication in Israel", with "Book a demo" as the primary CTA and public pricing. These are recommendations, not findings.*

### Positioning gaps to exploit

**Against Aircall**, position Call Pilot as "Israel-native", not "Aircall in Hebrew". Points to press: Hebrew that is two months old at Aircall and missing from its templates and AI Assist tiers; no Hebrew interface or Hebrew support team; USD pricing with a three-seat minimum and a stack of add-ons; Israeli calling treated as "international" with contact-sales rates; no Israeli data region; no Israeli integrations; reviews citing international call quality and slow support. Aircall is not a CRM, so Call Pilot's built-in customer and call dashboard is a structural difference, not a feature-list item. Borrow Aircall's own "specialist beats generalist" argument and apply it to Hebrew and Israel.

**Against local players**, position Call Pilot as "a product, not a project". Most of them are agencies or telephony incumbents with a bot added. Call Pilot can offer what none of them combine: a real CRM dashboard of calls and customers, self-serve-grade configuration with optional done-for-you onboarding, public shekel pricing, named Israeli customers, and a privacy story to match Daber's. Against Voicenter and Reshetcall in mid-to-large deals, the angle is AI-first scope (agent analytics, automations, MCP-style actions) rather than a PBX with a bot.

The "best AI voice caller in Hebrew" ambition only works if it is backed by evidence. Turn it into a public Hebrew quality benchmark, Call Pilot's version of Bland's Audio Realism Bench: blind A/B recordings of Call Pilot against Aircall's Hebrew agent and leading local agents on the same messy Israeli calls (slang, interruptions, background noise, names, addresses, ID numbers). Evaluate with ivrit.ai-style open methodology where possible. Arabic and Russian support alongside Hebrew, with natural code-switching, is a gap that nobody credibly claims.

### Claims to avoid as headlines

Do not lead with "native/natural Hebrew", "sounds human", "24/7", "never miss a call", "X% Hebrew accuracy", "under X ms", "live in minutes", "full CRM integration" or "AI secretary". Every Israeli and global competitor already says these, and the accuracy percentages (85%, 98%) are unverifiable and invite distrust. Use them only as supporting specs under a proof element. Also avoid "replaces your staff" (frame it as handoff with context instead), abstract enterprise slogans like "The future of CX", and Aircall's visual signature: deep green on cream with a serif accent. A Hebrew-first RTL type system and a distinct hue will set Call Pilot apart immediately. A light or warm style with real local business imagery reads as more SMB-friendly than the dark neon "AI lab" look.

### Demo and proof patterns to adopt

Since "Book a demo" is the primary CTA, the page must give visitors a reason to book, and must also give SMB visitors who won't book something to do. Recommended:

- **A live Israeli number in the hero** ("call our agent now") set up as a believable local business, such as a clinic or garage. This works as the secondary CTA alongside "Book a demo".
- **Post-call replay**: after the visitor calls, show what happened next. The transcript, summary and new customer record appear in the Call Pilot dashboard, and a WhatsApp or SMS confirmation arrives on their phone (Smith.ai pattern). No competitor ties call → CRM record in one visual. This is the signature demo.
- **Playable Hebrew recordings** per vertical, including deliberately messy calls (Fixer's "messy call" test), plus an optional Smith.ai-style AI-vs-human comparison.
- **The Hebrew benchmark** described above, published and repeatable.
- **An ROI calculator in ₪** (missed-call rate × lead value × hours not covered: mornings, evenings, Shabbat), as Parloa does next to "Book a demo".
- **Visible QA**: a test-suite panel ("40/40 test calls passed") and an explicit anti-hallucination statement. For example: "It only answers from your approved knowledge; otherwise it says it will check and hands off." Show it in a transcript. No one else names this objection.
- **The demo promise**: "we build your agent, in Hebrew, live on the call". This also matches the Israeli done-for-you buying habit.
- **Proof**: a few named Israeli customers with outcome numbers in ₪ and percentages will outweigh any competitor's anonymous avatars. An FAQ that mirrors Aircall's objection list (IVR vs AI, replacing the receptionist, do I need to change my phone system, setup time, languages, cost, security, training on my data).

### Recommended product scope: what Call Pilot must have to be credible against Aircall

| Area | Must have at launch | Can follow |
|---|---|---|
| Voice agents | Inbound and outbound in Hebrew (Arabic/Russian/English with switching as a differentiator); plain-language instructions; knowledge-base ingestion; test mode with test-call suites; warm transfer with summary; booking; outbound list campaigns with consent gating; Israeli number provisioning or forwarding of an existing number; Israeli business-hours and holiday calendars | Voice library and cloning; agent-to-agent memory; vertical templates library |
| Messaging | WhatsApp follow-up and missed-call → WhatsApp within seconds; shared memory across phone and WhatsApp | SMS and full WhatsApp AI agent |
| CRM dashboard | Unified timeline per customer (calls, recordings, transcripts, summaries, sentiment, tags, assignment, notes); call log with search and filters; per-agent KPIs (resolution rate, transfer rate, booked appointments, time saved, missed calls recovered) | Live monitoring; AI "ask your data" chat; scoring rubrics; copilot for human reps |
| Automations | Trigger → action rules (call ended / intent detected / no answer → WhatsApp, task, CRM update, webhook); calendar booking (Google/Outlook); Zapier/Make/n8n; API and webhooks | Visual workflow builder (an apparent Aircall gap); MCP actions |
| Integrations | Monday.com, Fireberry/Powerlink, HubSpot, Google Calendar, WhatsApp Business | Salesforce, Priority, Israeli invoicing tools (iCount/Green Invoice), Shopify |
| Trust and admin | Roles and permissions, 2FA, audit log, retention settings, Hebrew PII masking, data-erasure tools; Hebrew RTL UI | SSO/SAML, SOC 2/ISO 27001, Israeli or EU data residency, on-prem for regulated buyers (Daber's ground) |

Call Pilot does not need Aircall's full cloud PBX (seats, softphone, power dialer, IVR trees) to be credible with AI-first buyers. Aircall itself now sells the agent "with or without a phone system". Forwarding an existing number plus warm transfer to the business's existing lines covers SMBs. A human-agent workspace becomes necessary as the product moves toward mid-to-large accounts.

### Pricing-model implications

Publish prices in ₪ and state VAT clearly ("לא כולל מע"מ" or "כולל מע"מ"), with Israeli tax invoices. This alone separates Call Pilot from Yappr, Aircall and the agencies. For SMBs, use flat monthly tiers with bundled minutes and no seat minimum (the Rosie/Goodcall model, "a bill you can predict"), with clearly priced overage. Do not anchor the brand on a per-minute rate, because per-minute prices are falling fast. Reference points for converting: Aircall $0.29/min (roughly ₪1/min) and $250 for 1,000 minutes; Yappr $600–$997/month base; Bot10 reportedly ₪3,000 for setup plus 1,000 minutes; Rosie $149 for 1,000 minutes. Include the CRM dashboard and WhatsApp follow-up in every tier instead of selling them as add-ons, as a direct contrast to Aircall's add-on stack. Offer paid or included done-for-you onboarding as a visible line item, since Israeli buyers expect it. Keep a "Contact us" enterprise tier for volume, SSO, residency and SLAs, which fits the long-term mid-to-large vision. Consider a per-outcome component later (per booked appointment or per resolved call, following Sierra and Smith.ai). Validate the actual ₪ price points with buyer interviews; the research has no Israeli willingness-to-pay data.

### Compliance must-haves

**Product:**
- An AI-disclosure and recording notice at call start, on by default.
- Configurable retention and auto-deletion for recordings and transcripts.
- Hebrew PII masking (ID numbers, card numbers, phones).
- Erasure by phone number or customer, with an audit log.
- An outbound consent ledger: source, timestamp and recorded consent per number. Campaigns are blocked for numbers without consent unless the call is a service call. An in-call opt-out ("remove me") writes to a do-not-call list.
- No card capture by voice except through a secure DTMF or payment-link route.
- Records-of-processing and DPA documentation so customers can meet their own Amendment 13 duties.
- A stated data location and sub-processor list.
- Never-train-on-customer-data as the default, matching Aircall's promise.

**Landing page:**
- An IS 5568 / WCAG AA build with a published Hebrew accessibility statement and accessibility contact.
- A Hebrew privacy policy that addresses Amendment 13 (purposes, retention, data location, rights, privacy officer contact where applicable).
- Lead forms with a separate, unticked marketing-consent checkbox, kept apart from the demo request, to comply with the spam law.
- Cookie and analytics disclosure.
- If the live demo number records callers, the same recording and AI notice at the start of that call.
- A compact trust strip (privacy, data handling, Israeli company and support, certifications once real) rather than a full badge wall. Never display certifications Call Pilot does not hold.

Have Israeli counsel confirm the "automatic dialing system" interpretation and any AI-disclosure duty before launching outbound campaigns.
